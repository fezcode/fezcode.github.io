what:
 AOR — album-oriented rock — was a US radio format before it was a genre. Between roughly 1976 and 1986 it defined a sound: melodic hard rock with keyboard beds, multi-tracked harmony vocals and a guitar solo in the third minute. The Who predate it and sit in the older classic-rock lineage; Journey are its commercial peak; America represent its soft-rock, folk-descended wing.

artists:
 *The Who* (Townshend, Daltrey, Entwistle, Moon — with Kenney Jones drumming by 1982), *Journey* (Steve Perry's tenor over Neal Schon's guitar), *America* (Beckley, Bunnell, Peek), plus Boston, Foreigner, Toto, Styx, REO Speedwagon.

trivia:
 "Eminence Front" (1982) is one of the rare Who songs with *Pete Townshend, not Roger Daltrey, on lead vocal* — it's a sneer at cocaine-era self-delusion, sung by someone who was inside it. Journey's "Separate Ways" video, shot on the Astoria, Oregon docks with the band miming on painted-on instruments, was mocked for decades and then reclaimed as an artefact. And America's *Silent Letter* (1979) was produced by *George Martin*, who took over their records after Beatles duties ended and stayed for six albums.

sonic:
 Mid-forward and dense. Rhythm guitars chorused and doubled, keys filling every gap, snare with plate reverb and a long decay, and lead vocals compressed hard so they sit above a wall of harmony. "Eminence Front" is the outlier: it's built on a locked synth arpeggio and a clean funk guitar figure, closer to a groove record than a rock one.

gear:
 Fender Rhodes, Hammond B3, and by 1982 polysynths — Yamaha CS-80, Oberheim OB-Xa, Prophet-5 — for the arpeggios and pads. Guitars: Les Pauls and Strats into Marshall or Boogie stacks. Studios ran *Neve or SSL desks to 24-track 2-inch tape*, with EMT plate reverbs and the then-new Lexicon 224 digital reverb on snare and vocals.

prod:
 Everything is arranged around the chorus arriving in the first minute, because radio programmers auditioned records on a small speaker. Vocal stacks are recorded as three or four separate passes rather than copied, which is why the harmonies shimmer instead of phasing. The mix leaves a deliberate hole around 2–4 kHz for the lead vocal — the "AOR pocket" — and the bass is played with a pick for definition on car speakers.
