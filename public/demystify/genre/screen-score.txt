what:
 Film scoring split in two around 2000. One branch stayed orchestral and thematic; the other, driven by Hans Zimmer's Remote Control operation and by television's expansion, became texture-led — sustained tones, ostinatos and sound design rather than melody. Prestige television then created a parallel market where a score can define a series' identity more than its cast does.

artists:
 *Hans Zimmer* and Junkie XL; *Volker Bertelmann* (Hauschka), whose prepared-piano background reached _Conclave_ and _All Quiet on the Western Front_; Clint Mansell, Tyler Bates, Jonny Greenwood, Stephen Barton, Paolo Buonvino; *Lera Lynn*, who performed on camera in _True Detective_; and The Cinematic Orchestra at the crossover point.

trivia:
 Jonny Greenwood scores films while remaining Radiohead's guitarist, and his _There Will Be Blood_ music was ruled ineligible for an Oscar because it incorporated his own earlier concert work. Volker Bertelmann built his reputation on *prepared piano* — inserting felt, tape and metal between the strings — a technique from mid-century avant-garde composition that he brought into mainstream cinema.

sonic:
 Long sustained textures with slow harmonic movement, ostinato figures under dialogue, and a dynamic range far wider than any commercial genre — a cue may sit at conversational level for three minutes before a full orchestral entry. Melody is deliberately unobtrusive; a theme that draws attention is usually a fault.

gear:
 Live orchestras recorded at Abbey Road, AIR and increasingly Budapest and Prague for cost; sample libraries (Spitfire, Orchestral Tools) for mockups that frequently survive into the final mix; prepared piano, cimbalom and processed solo strings for texture; and heavy convolution reverb built from real scoring stages.

prod:
 The composer delivers stems, not a mix — the dub stage balances music against dialogue and effects, and the soundtrack album is a separate assembly made afterwards. That is why album versions of cues often have material you never hear in the film, and why they end abruptly: the cue was written to stop when the scene cut.
