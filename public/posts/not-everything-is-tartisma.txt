# Not Everything Is "Tartışma"

In Turkish today, *tartışmak* does almost all the work. A calm panel is a *tartışma*. So is a shouting match in traffic, a school debate, a scolding from your boss, and a review of a film. One word, five very different situations.

Ottoman Turkish had a separate word for each of them, and most are still in the dictionary. You don't have to sound like a 19th-century newspaper, but picking the right one says *what kind* of argument it was.

## The words

| Word | What it is | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Münakaşa**<br>مناقشه | A heated argument; quarrelling, sparring | Voices are raised and nobody is listening |
| **Münazara**<br>مناظره | A formal debate: two sides argue opposite views under rules | It's a school debate, a staged contest, a structured exchange |
| **Mübahase**<br>مباحثه | A discussion: talking a subject through together | People disagree, but calmly and in good faith |
| **Muahaze**<br>مؤاخذه | Reproach, reprimand; calling someone to account | One side is blaming the other for a fault |
| **Münazaa**<br>منازعه | A dispute or feud, worse than a münakaşa | It has turned into a real conflict |
| **Mücadele**<br>مجادله | A struggle; in Arabic, a disputation | Effort against resistance, not just words |
| **Münekkit**<br>منقد | A critic: someone whose job is criticism | You mean the person, not the act |

## Try it

- Two ministers on a TV panel, polite and prepared: **mübahase**, or **münazara** if there's a moderator and rules.
- Two neighbours yelling over a parking spot: **münakaşa**.
- The parking fight became a lawsuit that has run for years: **münazaa**.
- Your manager tells you off for a missed deadline: **muahaze**.
- The person who reviewed your novel in the paper: **münekkit**.

None of these are wrong to replace with *tartışma* in everyday speech. But "we had a *mübahase*" and "we had a *münakaşa*" tell two completely different stories, and *tartışma* tells neither.
