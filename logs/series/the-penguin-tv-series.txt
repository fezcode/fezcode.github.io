## About

**The Penguin (2024)** is HBO's eight-part Batman spin-off from Lauren LeFranc, with Colin Farrell's Oz Cobb clawing upward through a drowned Gotham. Cristin Milioti's Sofia Falcone is the real engine.

Episode four, [*Cent'anni*](/vocab/cent-anni), hands the whole hour to her: a father who buried his own murders under a daughter's diagnosis, ten years in Arkham for asking questions, and a family dinner she ends with a single word. It reframes everything before it.

## WTF Moments

> Spoilers. All of them.
>
> **Episode 4, "Cent'anni."** Sofia toasts a dining room full of Falcones she has already sealed in with gas, then walks out through them. Ten years in Arkham for asking one question about her mother, and she settles the whole account over a single dinner.
>
> **Episode 7, the tunnels.** Flashlight tag with Jack and Benny. Oz can't manage the ladder, decides his brothers did it to humiliate him, and shuts the overflow door on the both of them. Then he walks home, tells his mother they went to a movie, and curls up on the couch with her while it rains all night. He was a child. He drowned two children so he would be the only one left to love.
>
> **Episode 8, Francis.** Not dementia — psychosis. She knew exactly what he was the entire time. She tells him she hates him and puts a broken bottle in him before the stroke takes her.
>
> **Episode 8, Vic.** A bench by the water. The kid says "You're family to me" and means it, and Oz puts an arm around his shoulder and strangles him while he begs. "I'm sorry, kid." I sat there with my jaw open. I genuinely did not believe the show would do it — Vic was the one clean thing left in it, and that is precisely why he had to go.
>
> **The last shot.** Penthouse, top hat, Gotham at his feet. His mother is alive in a chair by the window, vegetative, a single tear on her face — and Eve is dressed as her on the night they went dancing, so Oz can hold the fantasy instead. The Bat-Signal comes up behind them.

## Oz

Colin Farrell is magnificent and Oz Cobb is repulsive. Not fun-repulsive, not a rogue you quietly root for — genuinely, skin-crawlingly disgusting. Every warm thing he does is a down payment on something he wants.

He murders two children for his mother's attention. He spends forty years building an empire as an apology he never actually makes. He keeps her breathing so she can witness it, then hires a stand-in to play her when she can't. And he strangles the only person who ever loved him, because being loved is a thing that can be held against you.

The show never once lets you enjoy him. That is the achievement, and it is why the finale lands like a punch rather than a twist.

**5/5.**
