what:
 New wave is what happened when punk's energy met people who could actually play and wanted a hit. By 1982 the term covered synth-pop, jangle guitar bands and the entire first generation of MTV. American college radio ran a parallel track — R.E.M., the Pixies, the Smiths' UK equivalent — that stayed off the mainstream charts and eventually produced everything called alternative in the 1990s.

artists:
 *Tears For Fears*, Spandau Ballet, Duran Duran, The Human League and Cyndi Lauper on the pop side; *The Smiths*, *R.E.M.* and the *Pixies* on the college-radio side; Germany's Sandra and Sweden's later revivalists represent the continental version.

trivia:
 The Pixies' "Where Is My Mind?" was written after Black Francis went scuba diving in the Caribbean and had a fish follow him around; it has since scored more films about mental collapse than any other song of its era. Tears For Fears took their name and much of their early lyrical material from *Arthur Janov's primal therapy*, which they had both undergone — "Everybody Wants to Rule the World" is the point where they stopped writing about it and got a number one.

sonic:
 Bright, chorused guitars playing arpeggios rather than chords; melodic bass well forward; gated snare with a short bright plate; and a synth pad filling everything between. Songs are short, the chorus arrives early, and harmony stays major-key and diatonic with one surprising borrowed chord — usually in the pre-chorus.

gear:
 Rickenbacker 330 and Fender Telecaster into Roland JC-120 chorus amps; Yamaha DX7, Roland Juno-106 and Oberheim OB-X for pads and bell tones; LinnDrum and Simmons pads layered under acoustic kits; and the era's signature — an AMS or Lexicon gated reverb on the snare.

prod:
 Recorded to 24-track analogue with gates keyed to the snare, guitars double-tracked and hard-panned, and the vocal compressed hard so it floats above a dense bed. The mix leaves a deliberate notch at 2–4 kHz for the voice. These masters are relatively quiet by modern standards, which is why 1980s reissues so often sound better than the loudness-war remasters that replaced them.
