what:
 "Boom bap" is onomatopoeia for the kick-snare pattern that defined East Coast rap from about 1988 to 1996 — DJ Premier, Pete Rock, Large Professor, RZA. Its afterlife has been unusually long: the Griselda camp out of Buffalo (Westside Gunn, Conway, Benny) revived it wholesale in the late 2010s, sometimes stripping the drums out entirely for a "drumless" variant.

artists:
 Gang Starr, Pete Rock & CL Smooth, A Tribe Called Quest, *De La Soul*, Wu-Tang Clan, Nas. The underground continuum: *MF DOOM*, Evidence (of Dilated Peoples, and a producer in his own right), The Alchemist, Roc Marciano, Ka, and Griselda. *ASM* (A State of Mind) is a French duo — DOOM guested widely with European acts in his later years.

trivia:
 The title track of De La Soul's *Stakes Is High* (1996) was the first beat the group took from a young Detroit producer named *J Dilla*, and it introduced him to a national audience. Separately, De La Soul's catalogue was *absent from all streaming services until March 2023* because of unresolved 1980s sample clearances — three decades of a foundational discography effectively invisible. MF DOOM's death in October 2020 was not announced until December 31 of that year.

sonic:
 Dusty, band-limited loops — usually a 2–4 bar chop from a soul, jazz or library record — with the drums sitting slightly behind or ahead of the grid rather than quantised flat. Kick around 60–80 Hz, snare thick and mid-heavy at 200 Hz with a short room tail. Griselda-era production pushes this further: loops left unlooped, tape hiss audible, sometimes no drums at all under the verse.

gear:
 The *Akai MPC60 and MPC3000* are the genre's defining instruments — their 12-bit converters and slightly loose internal timing produce the grit and the swing. Also the E-mu SP-1200 (even lower fidelity, prized for it), Technics 1200 turntables for the digging and the scratches, and a Roland TR-808/909 for supplementary drums.

prod:
 The signature swing is a sequencer setting, not a performance: the MPC's timing-correct/swing parameter set around 54–62% pushes every off-beat late by a few milliseconds. Producers deliberately record loops through the sampler's low bit-depth and sometimes through a cassette deck for saturation. Modern practitioners recreate this with plugins but many, Alchemist and Evidence included, still track through hardware because the artefacts are the point.
