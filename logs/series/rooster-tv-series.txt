## About

**Rooster (2026)** is a comedy from Bill Lawrence and Matt Tarses — the *Scrubs* pairing — starring Steve Carell as Greg Russo, a wildly successful author of trashy beach reads whose marriage has just collapsed under his own infidelity. Adrift, he takes a Writer in Residence post at the fictional Ludlow College, mostly as an excuse to be near his adult daughter Katie (Charly Clive), an art history professor there whose own marriage is falling apart at the same time. Ten episodes, premiered on HBO in March 2026.

## Thoughts

The cast is stacked — Carell, Danielle Deadwyler, Phil Dunster, Annie Mumolo — and that's most of what the show has going for it. Lawrence is running the formula he's run several times now: a damaged man, a found community that refuses to let him stay damaged, and a weekly ratio of jokes to sincerity calibrated to the decimal. The father-daughter material is the one part with a pulse — two people handling the same wound a generation apart, neither willing to admit to the other that they're not fine — and even that gets undercut by the show's need to land a joke every ninety seconds.

Everything around it is inert. It never decides what it's about; the campus is a backdrop rather than a subject, most of the supporting cast are visibly waiting for a season two to hand them a character, and a genuine share of the jokes feel transplanted from a sitcom made fifteen years ago. Deadwyler in particular is wasted. Carell can carry an enormous amount on charm alone, and here he's asked to carry all of it — the show gives him nothing to play, and coasts on the assumption that watching him be likeable is sufficient.

**2/5.** Not painful, just hollow — ten episodes of good actors doing warmed-over material in a show with no reason to exist. The pedigree promised something sharp; this is the formula on autopilot.
