23G-FUNK & WEST COAST RAP

Parliament basslines, a whining synth lead, and the Californian version of menace

WHAT IT IS / WHEN

Dr. Dre's The Chronic (1992) rebuilt West Coast rap around slow tempos, live-played Moog basslines and a high portamento synth lead, all lifted conceptually from Parliament-Funkadelic. The style dominated commercially for five years, splintered into Bay Area mobb music and hyphy, and remains the default sound of Californian rap thirty years later.

SIGNIFICANT ARTISTS

Dr. Dre, Snoop Dogg, Warren G, Tha Dogg Pound, Above The Law; the Bay Area lineage of Too $hort, Luniz, Rappin' 4-Tay, 3X Krazy, MC Breed and San Quinn; 2Pac across both; and the modern revival through YG, Mozzy, Larry June and the LNDN DRGS/Jay Worthy axis.

TRIVIA

Above The Law have a reasonable claim to inventing G-funk before The Chronic — their 1990 Livin' Like Hustlers, produced with Dre, has most of the elements already — and Cold 187um has said so publicly for three decades. The Luniz' "I Got 5 On It" is built on a sample of Club Nouveau's "Why You Treat Me So Bad," which is itself a reworking of a Timex Social Club song by the same people.

SONIC SIGNATURE

Tempos at 90–100 BPM, a synthesised bassline with heavy portamento playing an actual melodic figure, and a thin, nasal lead synth on top. Live bass, guitar and female backing vocals are common — these are band records disguised as rap records. The low end is enormous and tuned, because the music was mixed for car systems first.

KEY GEAR

Moog and Minimoog for the bass, Roland Juno and the ubiquitous whining lead patch, Akai MPC60 and SP-1200 for drums, plus session players on bass and guitar. The Bay Area records were often cut far cheaper on Ensoniq workstations, which gives them a distinctly harder, thinner character than the Los Angeles productions.

RATIONALE & PRODUCTION

Mixed explicitly for automotive playback: the bass is tuned to the resonance of a car cabin, the snare is bright enough to cut through road noise, and the vocal is compressed flat. Dre's method was to re-play samples with live musicians rather than clear them, which is why The Chronic sounds like a band and cost less in publishing than it should have.
